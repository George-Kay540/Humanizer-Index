Are There Any Reliable AI Humanizers?

The humanizer market is saturated with tools that promise to help you pass AI detector tests, but most are empty promises. Marketing copy and reality are two completely different things. One main reason why most AI humanizers fail is because they only swap words, for example, "utilize" with "use", "however" with "but", and so on. But that doesn't change the text patterns that a detector looks for. AI Detectors like Turnitin, GPTZero, Originality, and QuillBot AI Detector don't look at word choice. They look at perplexity (how likely each word is based on the previous words) and burstiness (how different the sentence length and structure are in the text). AI writing scores low on both of those metrics, and swapping out words doesn't alter that, which is why so many "humanized" essays, blogs, and articles are still caught by detectors and labeled "Likely paraphrased".

Based on our testing, the AI humanizers that work take a more robust approach by reconstructing sentences, paragraphs, and other aspects of text to vary sentence length, rhythm, and inject natural human flaws rather than basic word-level spinning on top of the AI's structure. This is the fine line between a tool that might be able to bypass an AI detector, and others that don’t stack up at all and still get you flagged, all while promising you that they can in their advertisements.

To effectively test the best AI humanizers, we generated an essay using ChatGPT, ran it through each humanizer with no edits, and checked the output against Turnitin, GPTZero, Originality, and QuillBot AI Detector, the most popular tools used by editors, professors, and publishers to review content. Unfortunately, most of the AI humanizers we tested failed since they relied on basic word spinning.
